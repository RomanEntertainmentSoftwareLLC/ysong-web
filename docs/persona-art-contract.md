# AI persona art contract

This contract governs finished AI persona portraits used in YSong. The canonical visual references are the production assets in [`public/ai-personas/`](../public/ai-personas/): `surfer-dude.png`, `goth-girl.png`, and `pop-princess.png`. Review all three at full size before creating, commissioning, replacing, or approving persona art. They define one shared illustration system with room for distinct character identities.

## Required visual language

- **Line and shape:** Use confident, clean, dark contour lines with varied weight. Silhouette, facial features, hair strands, clothing edges, and small accessories should read as deliberate inked shapes. Keep edges crisp; avoid sketch construction marks, loose scribbles, vector-flat uniform outlines, and fuzzy painterly edges.
- **Shading and texture:** Build form with cel-like shadow shapes plus refined painterly transitions, directional highlights, and selective fine texture. Hair, skin, fabric, and metal should have distinct, controlled surface treatment. Match the references' high-detail digital comic/editorial finish; avoid flat fills, airbrush-only modeling, noisy texture overlays, and photorealistic rendering.
- **Proportions:** Use expressive, appealing near-realistic adult proportions, with a large, legible head and face as the focal point. Faces are stylized but structurally coherent; eyes and expressions can be heightened without chibi, mascot, or caricature proportions. Show a head-and-shoulders/upper-chest portrait with enough wardrobe and signature accessories to identify the persona.
- **Facial design:** Give each character a readable, asymmetric expression and clear eye direction. Use shaped brows, defined eyelids/lashes where appropriate, a carefully modeled nose, and a distinct mouth. Features must remain legible at avatar size. Preserve character-specific presentation: relaxed, sunlit confidence for Surfer Dude; poised, darkly playful confidence for Goth Girl; bright, sparkling stage-ready warmth for Pop Princess. These are identity cues, not limits on who may be represented.
- **Color and light:** Use rich, intentional character palettes, warm dimensional skin tones, deep shadow values, and bright specular accents. Keep the face separated from hair and background. Color should feel saturated and polished without clipping, muddy shadows, or a generic one-palette treatment across every persona. Preserve clear contrast in small circular crops.
- **Framing:** Deliver a square, high-resolution portrait designed for a circular crop. Center the face, leave safe space around hair and silhouette, and include shoulders/upper chest. Keep essential facial features and identity cues inside the central crop-safe area; do not put important content at the corners. The references use a restrained, very dark circular backdrop that supports the portrait without competing with it. Match the consuming card/avatar crop and background behavior in the product UI.
- **Polish:** Finish the whole image, including hair edges, jewelry, clothing details, highlights, and background. No malformed anatomy, stray marks, accidental tangencies, clipped accessories, illegible details, or obvious generative artifacts. At thumbnail size the expression and silhouette must still read; at full size the rendering must withstand inspection.

## Production acceptance gate

An asset is production persona art only when it has been reviewed against all three canonical references and meets every required visual-language and framing point above. A persona may vary in skin tone, hair, styling, clothing, accessories, mood, and palette to express its identity, while retaining the shared linework, dimensional rendering, expressive portrait proportions, crop-safe composition, and finish.

Generic placeholder art is never final production persona art. This includes initials, emoji, stock avatars, generic profile silhouettes, uncustomized template illustrations, flat icon art, and rough or unfinished generated images. Such material may be used temporarily during implementation only when clearly marked as a placeholder and tracked for replacement. Do not approve a release or represent the persona as complete while placeholder art remains. Do not silently promote a temporary fallback, missing-image substitute, or loading state to the canonical persona asset.

When no approved portrait exists, keep the state visibly temporary in development and record the missing asset for replacement. Before release, verify that each persona entry points to its reviewed, finished portrait and that the actual avatar/card crop preserves its face and signature styling.
